How the model reads

Tokens

The model never sees letters or words, only tokens. Type anything and see how the model splits it.

Try:

Why some text costs more. The tokenizer reflects what the model was trained on. Text it saw less, such as names, Urdu and rare words, splits into more tokens: it uses more of the context window, and costs more on paid APIs. Try Jacob, then Zafar: five letters each, one token against three.

What am I looking at? Each box is one token, with its number underneath: the model only ever works with those numbers. ␣ is a space, which belongs to the word after it. Common words stay whole however long they are; rare ones split into pieces. English averages about 4 characters a token. This is Qwen's own tokenizer; other models split differently.

The vocabulary is fixed when the model is built. It is made before training, by merging the most frequent pieces of the training text until it reaches a chosen size; the model then learns one row of numbers per piece, and picks every next token from exactly this list. Adding a piece would mean changing the model and training it again.

      When does temperature matter, and why doesn't it stop mistakes?
      The bars are the odds for the next token, not for the answer. On Capital of France, “The” and “Paris” share
      the odds, yet both end in Paris: they differ in how the model answers, not what. On Random number the
      candidates are different answers, so temperature, top-k and top-p change what comes out.


      Temperature controls consistency, not truth. At 0 the model always takes its favourite token, so a wrong
      favourite is wrong every time. Top-k and top-p cut off the unlikely candidates; temperature reshapes the odds of
      the ones left. llama-server's extra min_p filter is turned off here so these are the only filters at work.
